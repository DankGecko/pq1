# Spec-Assurance Deep Research — 2026-06-11 (pass 1: theory + mutation testing)

> Adversarially verified (claims killed unless ≥2/3 independent checks confirmed against primary sources).
> Stats: 27 sources fetched, 134 claims extracted, 25 verified, 23 confirmed, 2 refuted.

## Summary

As of June 2026, the verified state of the art in spec assurance rests on two pillars that transfer directly to LeanLoop. First, the model-checking sanity-check literature (Kupferman–Vardi vacuity, coverage, and their proven duality) provides the formal foundation and strong empirical yield data (IBM: ~20% of specs pass vacuously on first runs, every vacuous pass a real problem) for LeanLoop's existing `vet` probes, and the 2025 Amazon FMCAD result shows the same coverage notion now runs in deductive verification via SMT unsat cores. Second, "mutate the implementation, expect the proof to fail" is mature, quantified prior art: mCoq (Coq, ICSE/ASE 2019-20) defines the exact live/killed proof-break scoring semantics with measured yield (92.37% average mutation score on mature libraries; ~85% of triaged surviving mutants were real spec gaps, ~15% equivalent mutants), and MutDafny (2025/26) replicates the rule for auto-active verification with a replicable 40-operator selection methodology mined from real bugfix commits. For LeanLoop's `mutate` command, cargo-mutants is verified as usable as a mutant generator (--list --json with diffs) and its four-outcome taxonomy maps cleanly onto proof-break scoring, but its kill criterion is hard-wired to `cargo test`, and its operator set lacks constant-tweaking mutations (e.g., domain-separator bytes) that matter most for crypto code — so LeanLoop must own the apply-and-judge loop and add custom operators. Research questions 3-5 (differential/KAT testing mechanics, LLM spec generation/critique including the Verification Facade taxonomy, and high-assurance project practice) produced no claims surviving adversarial verification and require a follow-up research pass before those roadmap items are designed.

## Verified findings

### 1. [high] Vacuity theory from temporal model checking is the formal foundation for LeanLoop's statement-level probes: vacuous satisfaction is formally defined as a subformula not affecting the spec's truth value (universally-quantified replacement preserves satisfaction); Kupferman–Vardi strengthened Beer et al.'s limited-ACTL-fragment notion to check EVERY subformula across all of CTL*; the canonical example is antecedent failure in AG(req → AF grant), remedied by an 'interesting witness' — the exact model-checking analogue of LeanLoop's hypothesis-satisfiability witness search. The motivating premise — that a POSITIVE verification answer should itself be suspected because spec/modeling errors hide behind successful checks — is the same residual-trust argument that motivates spec assurance for proofs that succeed in Lean.

**Evidence:** Kupferman & Vardi (CHARME 1999/STTT 2003): 'Our definition of vacuity is stronger, in the sense that we check whether all the subformulas of the specification affect its truth value in the system'; 'we present a general method for detection of vacuity and generation of interesting witnesses for specifications in CTL*'. Kupferman (CONCUR 2006), Definition 2: 'A system K satisfies a formula φ vacuously iff K ⊨ φ and there is some subformula ψ of φ such that ψ does not affect φ in K.' The req/grant antecedent-failure example and interesting-witness remedy are verbatim in the primary source. Verifiers confirmed all quotes verbatim against extracted PDF text. Caveat: the ITP transfer is analogical (Kripke-structure-relative definitions vs Lean hypothesis vacuity), and the 'exact analogue' framing holds for the canonical antecedent-failure case, which is precisely what LeanLoop's witness search targets.

- https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~ornak/publications/concur06b.pdf
- https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-48153-2_8

### 2. [high] Vacuity detection is empirically high-yield, not just theoretically sound: IBM's industrial experience (Beer et al.) found roughly 20% of specifications pass vacuously during first formal-verification runs of new hardware designs, and every vacuous pass pointed to a real problem in the design, spec, or environment. This is the strongest available empirical justification for prioritizing LeanLoop's vacuity probes in the assurance-per-effort ranking.

**Evidence:** Verbatim from Kupferman (CONCUR 2006), citing Beer, Ben-David, Eisner, Rodeh (IBM Haifa, FMSD 2001): 'typically 20% of specifications pass vacuously during the first formal-verification runs of a new hardware design, and... vacuous passes always point to a real problem in either the design or its specification or environment.' Verifier cross-checked IBM's 2015 follow-up (Ben-David & Eisner, FMSD), which reaffirms that original antecedent-failure vacuity 'always indicates real problems' while noting generalized vacuity definitions are noisier. Caveat: one company's late-1990s hardware experience; the 'always' is definition-sensitive; extrapolation to ITP/Lean hypothesis-vacuity should be flagged as such to the human reviewer.

- https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~ornak/publications/concur06b.pdf

### 3. [high] Vacuity checking (mutate the spec) and coverage checking (mutate the system) are algorithmically the same problem — both repeat verification on a mutant input — and coverage is formally defined by system mutation: a state is covered iff flipping a variable there renders the spec untrue, with uncovered components being parts of the implementation the spec says nothing about. This duality is the theoretical lineage that makes 'mutate the Rust implementation, expect the Lean proof to fail' a principled spec-strength metric rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.

**Evidence:** Verbatim from the CONCUR 2006 abstract: 'the two checks are essentially the same: both are based on repeating the verification process on some mutant input. In vacuity, mutations are in the specifications, whereas in coverage, mutations are in the system.' Coverage Definition 3 [HKHZ99]: a state w is q-covered by φ iff K satisfies φ but the q-flipped mutant does not. Verifier confirmed the interpretive bridge via mCoq (identical operation on Coq projects) and 'Mutation Coverage Estimation for Model Checking' (ATVA 2004) connecting the literatures. Caveat: the paper's scope is finite-state temporal-logic model checking; 'Lean proof fails on mutated Rust' is an analogical transfer (proof failure ≠ statement falsity), so cite as lineage, not formal equivalence.

- https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~ornak/publications/concur06b.pdf

### 4. [high] The coverage/vacuity notion transfers to deductive software verification: Amazon's FMCAD 2025 work computes the same coverage metric via SMT unsatisfiable cores in Boogie/Dafny, detecting vacuous proofs, redundant specifications, and unconstrained implementation code, and ships in Dafny as --warn-contradictory-assumptions / --warn-redundant-assumptions. This is the closest existing analogue to an automated 'which hypotheses are doing no work' analysis for auto-active verifiers; no equivalent exists for Lean/ITP, where LeanLoop's mutation + vet probes are the available substitutes.

**Evidence:** Verbatim from the paper: 'the same notion of coverage used in model checking can be applied to deductive software verification, with a reasonable performance penalty when using an implementation based on unsatisfiable cores... identifying specification gaps by detecting vacuous proofs, unconstrained code, and unnecessary or redundant specifications.' Verifier confirmed the duality citation chain to Kupferman/Li/Seshia, the Dafny flag shipping (dafny.org blog 2023-10-27), and overhead numbers (~20-73% time). Caveats: unsat cores are non-minimal so detection is sound-but-incomplete; demonstrated for SMT-based auto-active verification, not ITP/Lean — a Lean adaptation would be novel work. Note: a related claim that this paper positions unsat cores as replacing the mutation-based measurement mechanism of the original Kupferman line was REFUTED (1-2) and must not be cited.

- https://www.amazon.science/publications/static-coverage-in-deductive-software-verification

### 5. [high] mCoq (ASE 2019 / ICSE 2020) is the first and most direct prior art for a LeanLoop `mutate` command: it mutates Coq definitions (the implementation — explicitly NOT specs or proofs, in analogy with mutation testing not mutating tests), exhaustively re-checks all proofs per mutant, and uses proof-based scoring semantics — a mutant is 'live' if every proof still checks and 'killed' if at least one fails — with the authors explicitly framing live mutants as a signal of specification incompleteness rather than proof inadequacy.

**Evidence:** README verbatim: 'mCoq applies a set of mutation operators to Coq definitions, generating modified versions, called mutants, of the project'; 'If all proofs of a mutant are successfully checked, a mutant is declared live; otherwise it is declared killed'; 'live mutants may indicate incomplete specifications.' ICSE 2020 paper §2: 'in analogy with mutation testing, which does not mutate tests, we do not change specification properties or proofs.' Corroborated by peer-reviewed ASE 2019 (Celik et al.) and ICSE 2020 (Jain et al.) papers; the ICSE paper lists designing mutation operators for Lean as future work. Caveats for the port: the tool is dormant (last release May 2020); non-elaborating mutants must be excluded from the live/killed tally rather than counted as killed; mCoq mutates Gallina directly whereas LeanLoop mutates Rust upstream of Aeneas extraction — analogous, not identical.

- https://github.com/EngineeringSoftware/mcoq
- https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3377812.3382156

### 6. [high] mCoq's quantitative results calibrate expectations for proof-break mutation scoring: across 12 mature Coq libraries (MathComp, Flocq, TLC, StructTact, etc.) it generated 5,311 mutants, killed 4,929, with mutation scores 76.50%–97.39% (avg 92.37%) — so even heavily-proved codebases leave roughly 3–24% of injected bugs undetected by their specs. Manual triage of 74 live mutants found 33 incomplete specs, 30 omitted specs, and 11 (~15%) semantically equivalent mutants — i.e., ~85% of survivors pointed at real spec gaps. The mutation score (killed / syntactically distinct mutants) indicates spec weakness 'intuitively, but not unambiguously' per the authors — a heuristic signal requiring triage, which dictates how LeanLoop should present mutate scores to a non-Lean-reading human reviewer.

**Evidence:** All numbers verified verbatim against the authors' PDF: 'The total number of generated, killed, and live mutants are 5311, 4929, and 382'; 'Mutation score varies between 76.50% (TLC) and 97.39% (FCSL PCM), and is 92.37% on average'; '33 indicate incomplete specifications, 30 indicate omitted specifications, and 11 are semantically equivalent'; 'A low mutation score intuitively, but not unambiguously, indicates that specifications may be incomplete.' Verifier caveats: the 74 triaged mutants were stratified, not randomly sampled (≥10% per operator/project plus all MathComp survivors), so 85% is an estimate for the inspected sample; numbers are from Coq 8.9/8.10 (2019-20); 92.37% is a macro-average. Design consequence: LeanLoop mutate output should list surviving mutants individually with diffs for triage, never just a headline percentage.

- https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3377812.3382156

### 7. [high] MutDafny (arXiv 2511.15403, v2 Apr 2026) is the current state of the art for the auto-active world and supplies both the scoring rule and the operator-selection methodology for a LeanLoop `mutate` command: a mutant that still verifies against the original spec is flagged as evidence of a weak spec, and its 40 mutation operators were chosen by filtering 147 operators from popular mutation tools across six languages down to 30 applicable ones, plus synthesizing 10 language-tailored operators from 1,475 real bugfix commits across 112 public Dafny GitHub repositories — a concrete, replicable recipe LeanLoop can repeat against Rust crypto/parser bugfix corpora.

**Evidence:** Abstract verbatim: 'If a program with a mutant verifies, this may indicate a weakness in the specification'; 'We extensively analyze mutation operators from popular tools, identifying the ones applicable to Dafny. In addition, we synthesize new operators tailored for the language from bugfix commits... a total of 40 mutation operators.' Evaluated on 794 real-world Dafny programs; triage of survivors found ~1 weak real-world spec per 241 LOC. Verifier caveats: recent preprint (one fetch suggested ICSE 2026 acceptance, unconfirmed); v1 said 32 operators, revised to 40 in v2; Dafny's SMT auto-verification differs from Lean re-elaboration, so kill-judging cost profiles differ; surviving mutants can be equivalent mutants and need manual triage ('may indicate').

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15403

### 8. [high] cargo-mutants is verified suitable as the mutant GENERATOR for LeanLoop but not as the kill JUDGE: (a) its four-outcome taxonomy (caught / missed / unviable / timeout) maps directly onto proof-break scoring — caught→'proof broke', missed→'spec too weak OR equivalent mutant', unviable (doesn't compile) must be scored inconclusive; (b) its kill criterion is hard-wired to `cargo test` (or nextest) with no arbitrary per-mutant command, so the Aeneas-extract-then-lake-build pipeline cannot plug in directly — LeanLoop must either wrap the Lean pipeline in a shelling-out cargo test, or use `cargo mutants --list --json` (diffs always included) purely as a generator and own the apply/judge loop; (c) its operators (FnValue body replacement, binary-operator swaps incl. ==↔!= and <→<= off-by-ones, unary deletion, match-arm deletion/guard replacement, struct-field deletion) cover boundary/off-by-one bugs via operator swaps but contain NO constant-tweaking mutations — changing a domain-separator byte, an ADRS type constant, or a length field is outside its operator set, and those are exactly the highest-value mutations for the SPHINCS+C10 and parser code, so LeanLoop needs custom crypto-aware operators.

**Evidence:** Verified against the official book and source current to v27.1.0 (2026-06-02): outcome definitions verbatim from mutants.rs/using-results.html ('No test failed with this mutation applied, which seems to indicate a gap in test coverage'; unviable 'is inconclusive about test coverage'); limitations.html verbatim: 'cargo-mutants currently only supports mutation testing of Rust code that builds using cargo and where the tests are run using cargo test'; src/options.rs defines only TestTool::{Cargo, Nextest}; mutants.html enumerates the full operator set and the deliberate exclusion 'Equality operators are not currently replaced with comparisons like < or <= because they are too prone to generate false positives'; PR #501/#503 (merged 2025-03-02) added <→<= / >→>= swaps; no literal/constant mutation operator exists in docs, changelog, or releases through mid-2026. For no_std suitability: the generator path (--list --json + external judging) sidesteps the cargo-test dependency entirely, which also avoids any no_std test-harness friction.

- https://mutants.rs/

## Summary

## Verified findings

### P2.1 [high] A1 — Mechanism: verified-ledger's differential testing is an in-process compiled-native FFI oracle (not #eval, not a subprocess). build.rs invokes `lake lean VerifiedLedger/FFI.lean -- -c` to compile the Lean model to C, then cc::Build compiles that emitted C plus the Lake-generated IR (`.lake/build/ir/VerifiedLedger/Model.c`) and a hand-written shim (`src/lean_wrapper.c`) into the Rust binary, statically linking Init, Std, leanrt, gmp, and uv. Lean entrypoints are exposed via `@[export]` attributes (e.g. `@[export verified_ledger_apply_deposit]` in lean/VerifiedLedger/FFI.lean) and called from Rust via `extern "C"` declarations in src/lean_model.rs, passing Lean state as an opaque `*mut c_void` with explicit refcount release (`ledger_lean_state_dec` → `lean_dec_ref`) and a `std::sync::Once`-guarded one-time `ledger_lean_initialize()`. Inputs come from a custom seeded XorShift64 PRNG (45% deposit / 30% withdraw / 25% transfer; zero runtime deps, no proptest/arbitrary); after EVERY step the harness compares both the ok/error result AND a full balance-snapshot of model vs implementation, and failures emit the recorded history plus a deterministic replay command (`cargo run -- replay --seed ... --steps ...`). The harness is small, dependency-free, and directly reusable as a reference architecture for LeanLoop.

**Evidence:** README: "The Rust build will invoke `lake` to compile the Lean model to C and link it into the binary, so make sure `lake` is on your PATH". FFI.lean: "@[export verified_ledger_apply_deposit] def ffiApplyDeposit (s : State) (account : String) (amount : UInt64) : ApplyResult := applyOp s (Op.deposit account amount)". fuzz.rs: "let results_match = model_result == impl_result; let state_match = model_snapshot == impl_snapshot; if !results_match || !state_match { return Err(FuzzFailure {". Verifiers reproduced build.rs link lines (`cargo:rustc-link-lib=static=Init/Std/leanrt/gmp/uv`), the Once-guarded init, Drop-based lean_dec_ref, and the exact 45/30/25 roll logic from the primary repo.

### P2.6 [high] C1 — The refinement theorem makes KAT-testing transitive: the Aeneas ML-KEM correctness theorem connects the extracted code model to the executable spec via abstraction function toPoly as a Hoare-style triple — `theorem Code.ntt.spec (f : Std.Array U16 256#usize) (h : inBounds f) : Code.ntt f {{ f1 => toPoly f1 = Spec.ntt (toPoly f) ∧ inBounds f1 }}` (i.e. toPoly (Code.ntt f) ≃ Spec.ntt (toPoly f) under inBounds). Because the executable spec is the proof target, any KAT testing of Spec.ntt transitively grounds the verified code — modulo the correctness of toPoly, the inBounds precondition actually holding, and Aeneas translation faithfulness (the Rust binary additionally trusts the extraction). This is the exact architecture LeanLoop should replicate.

**Evidence:** Slide 31 verified verbatim from the primary PDF: "toPoly (Code.ntt f) ≃ Spec.ntt (toPoly f)", "def inBounds (a : Std.Array U16 256#usize) := ∀ i < 256, a[i].val < 3329", and the full Code.ntt.spec triple. (2) Question B1 is only partially answered: the 'Verification Facade' document (Symbolic Software, on Cryspen's Hax pipeline) was located via citation [23] in the RV paper but its exact taxonomy was never extracted from the primary document. (3) Several sub-questions produced NO surviving claims and remain unanswered by this pass: HACL*/EverCrypt's spec-level KAT mechanism, fiat-crypto/EverParse harnesses (A3/C1), SpecGen/AutoSpec/SAFE/Verina/SpecEval error rates (B2), quantified multi-agent adversarial spec-review results (B3), seL4/CompCert spec-validation practice (C2), and Lean 4 KAT-anchored spec prior art in mathlib-adjacent crypto (C3) — the closest verified anchor for C3 feasibility is the Aeneas ML-KEM team's 'we test our specification on official test vectors... not super fast but it works'. (4) Time/strength sensitivities: nl2postcond numbers are GPT-4-era (FSE 2024) and likely stale lower bounds for current models; Clover's zero-false-positive result is on 60 textbook Dafny programs with author-constructed adversarial variants (precision 6/9 on real rejections); Son Ho's KAT statement comes from a YouTube auto-transcript (verbatim-verified, but 'official test vectors' is his phrasing, NIST is inferred); the canonical SymCrypt Aeneas proof development is internal — Symcrust.lean on the public branch is 'copied from experimental repo'. (5) For LeanLoop specifically: official SLH-DSA KATs cover standard parameter sets, not the project's custom C10 parameters — byte-equality anchoring for C10 will need vectors generated from the reference Rust crate (which weakens the 'external truth' property to cross-implementation agreement) plus standard-parameter-set runs of the shared spec components where FIPS-205 vectors do apply.

## Refuted (do NOT reuse)

- The paper describes NO differential-testing, FFI-oracle, or executable-spec testing mechanism for checking the Lean model against production Rust code. Its pipeline has exactly four stages (Rust-to-Lean extraction; specification; proof; kernel re-check), and the sole trust boundary is the Lean kernel re-checking proofs. Specifications are validated only by being handwritten 'against the extracted code' or stated as equivalence to ArkLib/CompPoly definitions — there is no execution of the Lean model on concrete inputs, no input generation, no result comparison against the Rust binary, and no KAT/test-vector validation anywhere in the paper.
